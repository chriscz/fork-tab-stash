## Problem

One or two sentences. What is broken, and for whom? If it takes more, you haven't found the problem — you've found a topic.

## Background

Only what a reader needs to evaluate the proposal. Link out for the rest.

## Goals

What this must achieve to be worth doing.

## Non-goals

What we are explicitly not solving here. Be specific — "we are not replacing X" beats "scope is limited."

## Proposal

The design. Enough detail that a competent engineer on the team could start implementing. Examples, not adjectives.

## Alternatives considered

Each one: what it was, why we rejected it. One paragraph. If you can't name any, you haven't thought about it hard enough.

## Tradeoffs and risks

What we're giving up, and what could go wrong. Not "it takes effort" — real tradeoffs. If you can't name three, ask someone who disagrees with you.

## Rollout

How we ship this without breaking things. Migration, feature flags, deprecation, backfills. "Big bang" is a valid answer; "we'll figure it out" is not.

## Success criteria

How we know it worked. Metric, behavior, or capability — something observable.

## Security, privacy, operational impact

One paragraph each, or "no impact" with a sentence of why. No skipping.

## Open questions

What we don't know yet. Mark each as: resolve in RFC / resolve in implementation / defer.
